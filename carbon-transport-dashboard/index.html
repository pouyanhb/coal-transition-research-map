What the national trend shows

Road travel per resident in 2024 was close to its 2000 level, while reported highway fuel used per 1,000 vehicle miles was lower. That suggests the long-run decline in fuel use per resident is more closely tied to lower fuel use per mile than to a sustained decline in road travel.

2024 note: FHWA fuel reporting shows a larger-than-usual change in 2024, so one-year fuel-intensity movements should be interpreted cautiously.

Method & definitions

What these measures mean

Road travel per resident (VMT per capita)

FHWA vehicle miles traveled on public roads divided by resident population. It includes cars, trucks, buses, motorcycles, freight and visitor traffic. It is best read as state road-travel intensity relative to population, not literal miles driven by the average resident.

Highway fuel per 1,000 VMT

FHWA highway gasoline plus highway special fuel divided by FHWA road VMT. Lower values mean fewer reported gallons are used for the same amount of road travel. The measure reflects vehicle efficiency, vehicle mix, freight activity and other system-level factors; it is not the same as passenger-vehicle MPG.

Gasoline consistency ratio

FHWA highway gasoline divided by EIA transportation-sector motor gasoline. EIA's transportation gasoline measure is broader than highway-only gasoline, so the ratio is a reconciliation check rather than a literal “share of transportation on highways.”

Why FHWA and EIA are both used

FHWA is the core source for what happens on public roads: VMT and highway fuel use. EIA provides the broader transportation-energy context. This first website version uses EIA only for the gasoline consistency comparison; the broader transportation CO₂ analysis will be added after Level 3 is completed.

Project data

FHWA MF-21 highway motor-fuel use; FHWA VM-2 vehicle miles traveled; EIA SEDS motor gasoline; EIA SEDS population. Historical backup fills from FHWA MF-225 and MF-226 are retained where the main MF-21 series was missing.
